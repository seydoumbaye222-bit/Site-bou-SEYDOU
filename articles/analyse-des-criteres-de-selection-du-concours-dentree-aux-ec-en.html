Analysis of the Selection Criteria for the Entrance Exam to Private Engineering Schools Post‑Reform 2024

← Retour à l'accueil

Since the 2024 reform of the admission system for private engineering schools, the selection methods have been profoundly revised, requiring candidates and institutions to adapt to new, more diversified and demanding evaluation criteria.

Context of the 2024 Reform

The 2024 reform aims to harmonize admissions while strengthening the dimension of transversal competencies. It introduced a common national framework, while allowing private schools a degree of flexibility to highlight their pedagogical specificities and professional requirements.

The New Exam Components

The exam now includes three mandatory tests—mathematics, physics‑chemistry, and logic—each scored out of 20 points, as well as an optional French or English test. This diversification aims to assess the candidate’s ability to mobilize multiple scientific and linguistic skills.

Weighting of Scores and Academic Criteria

The test scores are weighted variably by school: 40 % for mathematics, 30 % for physics‑chemistry, 20 % for logic, and 10 % for language. This distribution emphasizes core subjects while maintaining the importance of logical rigor and proficiency in a foreign language.

Importance of the Personal File and Interviews

Beyond the scores, the candidate’s file (transcripts, projects, internships) accounts for 30 % of the final decision. Interviews, meanwhile, assess motivation, argumentative ability, and professional vision—criteria now essential for admission to a private engineering school.

Impact on Candidates and Institutions

The new selection grid favors balanced profiles capable of combining academic excellence with practical experience. Schools, for their part, see a better match between admitted students and labor market expectations, thereby strengthening their attractiveness and success rates.

Conclusion

In summary, the 2024 reform has turned the entrance exam for private engineering schools into a more comprehensive process, combining test performance, file quality, and interview aptitude, thus providing a fairer selection more aligned with the needs of the technological sector.
